## What we copy

### Brownstone row house (UWS, Harlem, Park Slope type)
- Lot 16–25 ft (4.9–7.6 m) wide, typically 20 ft (6.1 m); 3–5 storeys; three bays (four on wide lots).
- English basement half below grade; **parlour floor 1.5–1.9 m above the sidewalk** reached by a
  stoop of 8–10 risers (~19 cm rise, 30 cm tread), solid stone cheek walls with sloped coping,
  cast-iron handrails with balusters and a newel post at the foot.
- Parlour windows tallest (~2.5 m), upper floors ~1.9 m, top floor shorter (~1.6 m);
  width ~1.05 m. One-over-one double-hung sashes, some with muntins.
- Italianate window **hoods**: architrave surround, frieze, projecting cap on small consoles;
  sills on corbels. Rusticated (banded) brownstone at basement/parlour level.
- Front door deeply recessed (~0.5 m) under a hood on consoles; double wooden doors, transom.
- Bracketed wooden/pressed-metal cornice ~0.8–1 m tall, painted dark brown/black, 3–5 brackets.
- Iron areaway fence ~1 m tall in front of the basement, 1.5–2 m out from the facade.

### Old-law tenement (East Village / LES)
- 25 ft (7.6 m) wide, 5–6 storeys, **four bays**; floor-to-floor ~3.0 m; windows ~0.95 × 1.75 m.
- Brick (red, buff, painted) with stone or painted lintels (flat with keystone, segmental arch,
  or hood) and projecting sills; sometimes sill-level belt courses.
- Ground floor: 1–2 shops, ~4.2 m tall, with cast-iron piers; residential door in the middle.
- **Fire escape** over two bays: balcony at every floor (≈1.1 m deep), slatted floor, railing
  ~0.95 m with balusters at ~12 cm, steep stair (≈60°) running along the facade between
  balconies (alternating direction), a counter-balanced drop ladder stowed under the lowest
  balcony, gooseneck ladder over the parapet. Mostly black; some green or red.
- Pressed-metal cornice ~1–1.2 m: frieze with panels, brackets every ~1.5 m, dentils, corona.
- Window AC units: roughly 1 in 5 windows, sitting on the sill, ~0.65 × 0.4 m, sticking out
  ~0.35 m, side accordion panels, beige/white/grey.

### Cast-iron loft (SoHo)
- 5–6 storeys, tall floors (~4 m); facade of painted cast iron (cream, grey, off-white,
  grey-green) imitating stone; **half-columns with capitals between every bay** on every floor
  and an entablature band at each floor line; bays ~3 m, windows ~2.1 × 2.8 m (two-over-two).
- Heavy bracketed cornice; fire escapes on many; ground floor raised ~0.5 m (loading
  platform with steps), tall storefront glass between columns, roll-down gates.

### Pre-war apartment house (UWS, West End Ave, Riverside Dr)
- 10–16 storeys; **2-storey rusticated limestone base**, brick shaft (buff, tan, red), limestone
  belt courses and quoins, top storey(s) with arched windows, a heavy modillioned cornice
  or Art Deco stepped crown; water tank on the roof in a timber tank on a steel stand.
- Entrance under a fabric canopy that runs from the door to the kerb (brass poles).

### Rooftops
- Parapet ~1 m with stone coping; tar roof; stair bulkhead (~2.6 × 3.2 × 2.7 m); brick
  chimneys along party walls on walk-ups; condenser units, vents, skylights.
- **Water tower**: cedar staves (vertical boards) with 5–7 steel hoops, diameter 3–4 m,
  height 3.6–4.6 m, conical roof with finial, on a steel stand 2.5–4 m tall (4 or 6 legs,
  cross-braced), with a ladder.

### Storefronts
- Height ~4–4.5 m. From the top: sign band/fascia (~0.9 m), sometimes a roll-down gate
  housing (~0.35 m) under it, transom glass (~0.5 m), display windows on a bulkhead
  (0.35–0.6 m), cast-iron or painted pilasters at each side.
- Entry often **recessed 0.6–1.3 m** with glazed side returns and a tiled floor; aluminium or
  wood glass door.
- Signs: painted fascia boards, internally lit box signs (white face, coloured letters),
  individual letters, projecting blade signs; neon/LED "OPEN", "ATM", "LOTTO" in windows.
- Awnings: sloped fabric with valance carrying the address or the name, or a box awning
  with the name; projects 1–1.5 m.
- Roll-down gates: corrugated steel slats (~10 cm), guide rails at the sides, often with
  graffiti or painted murals; shut gates are common in the day too.

### Glass towers
- Curtain wall with mullions every ~1.5 m, spandrel bands at floor lines, setbacks; double-
  height lobby (~7 m) between stone piers, revolving door, canopy.
