# Museum display research

What real museums do with objects of very different sizes, and what the museum mode copies.
Images were downloaded and looked at; all are on Wikimedia Commons.

## What we copy

**Everything is real size.** The display follows the object's measured size (after the realScale
correction), never the other way round. Then the room is sized to fit the displays.

| Real size | Display | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| aircraft longer than 1.5 m (hangar rooms) | hung from three steel cables at 4–9 m, slight bank, along the hall's length | Udvar-Hazy |
| longer than 1.9 m or taller than 2 m | on the floor or a low platform (0–0.24 m) with a margin of ~12 % of its size; barrier by room type: rope + brass posts (period, motor, maritime), steel rail (hangar), low glass wall (motor), yellow floor line (machine), bare edge (white cube) | Udvar-Hazy, Science Museum, V&A |
| boats | on two timber saddles on the platform | maritime museums |
| flat things (≤ 25 cm tall) | flat **table case**: 0.86 m cabinet, 0.3 m glass hood, grid of objects | V&A "Glazes" cases |
| thin upright things (guitars, swords, shields) in treasury / period / white rooms | **wall-mounted** at eye height (centre 1.5 m) with a label beside and a wash of light | instrument / armoury galleries |
| 0.75–1.9 m | open **plinth** (height falls as the object grows), **stepped riser** of 2–3 tiers (tallest at the back) or a **cluster of plinths of different heights** | MoMA, Vitra, V&A |
| small (< 0.75 m) | per room: **wall cases** (0.65 m cabinet base, glass to 2.4 m, glass shelves, light in the canopy), **open storage** cabinets (3.6 m long, 2.6 m tall, mullions), **glass towers** (free-standing, 2.4 m) or a **pedestal with a glass hood** | V&A ceramics, Pitt Rivers |
| the room's first object | the hero: its own row in the middle of the room, the brightest spot | every museum |

**Room archetypes** (by collection), each with its own wall colours, floor, ceiling and light:

- hangar (aircraft): barrel vault of white ribs or trusses, concrete / terrazzo, bright.
- motor (vehicles): dark walls, truss or flat ceiling, spotlit platforms.
- maritime (watercraft): dark blue / timber, tall, cradles.
- machine (industrial, power tools, assemblies): brick or grey walls, trusses or a roof lantern, floor lines.
- period (furniture, architecture, containers, bathroom): deep red / green / blue walls, parquet / marble / carpet, coffered ceilings or a lantern, dado and cornice, risers and ropes.
- white cube (food, toys, electronics, appliances, sport, packaging): white walls, pale oak / concrete, flat ceiling with downlights, plinth landscapes, hoods.
- treasury (instruments, lighting, arms, measuring): very dark walls, carpet / oak, objects in pools of spotlight, wall mounts.
- study (CAD parts, fasteners, hand tools, stationery, tableware): grey-green walls, parquet / tiles, lantern roof, open storage and table cases.
- science (articulated mechanisms): deep blue, terrazzo, lit plinths.
- open air (buildings): lawn, gravel paths, brick park wall with gateways, sky and sun.
- glasshouse (garden & outdoor): pitched glass roof on white ribs.

**Light.** Track rails across the room in front of each row of displays with black track cans aimed
at the objects; a limited number of real (shadowless) SpotLights go to the hero and the biggest
displays (12 in dark rooms, 5 in bright ones), everything else gets an emissive lens and an additive
**light pool** on the floor. Cases light themselves (emissive strip in the canopy). Bright rooms add
one to three soft fill lights.

**Signage.** Room title as vinyl lettering on the far wall; an introduction panel by the entrance;
a tombstone label per object (title, maker, real dimensions, "actual size") on a lectern, the plinth
front or the case's label rail; door signs over every exit naming the next room.

**Architecture.** Rooms vary in width, length, height and proportions (each archetype has its own
aspect range), doorway patterns (three exits on the far wall, or an enfilade axis plus side doors,
or staggered side exits), skirting / dado / cornice / shadow gaps, and benches in larger rooms.
