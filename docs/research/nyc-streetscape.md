# NYC streetscape: street furniture and street trees

References gathered from Wikimedia Commons (photos viewed during modelling), plus NYC DOT / Parks
standards from memory where noted. Dimensions are what the models in `src/nyc/streetprops.ts`,
`src/nyc/streettrees.ts` and `src/nyc/propatlas.ts` are built to.

## Street lights

- Bishop's Crook (historic districts, Greenwich Village, parks):
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NYC_Manhattan_6th_Avenue_Bishop_crook_lamppost.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ornately-framed_window_(and_air_conditioners)_and_a_Bishop%27s_Crook_lamppost_West_9th_Street,_Greenwich_Village,_New_York_City_(23163559466).jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1_Riverside_Drive_2008_jeh.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frauncestavern.JPG
  - Black cast iron. Fluted base ~1.2 m tall, ~0.5 m across; slender shaft (r ≈ 5–7 cm) with collar
    rings; at ~4.3 m the shaft turns into a hook ("crook") ~1 m out with a small scroll beneath; a
    teardrop / acorn luminaire hangs from the hook, light at ~3.9–4.2 m.
- Standard avenue/street light (most of Manhattan): galvanised or grey tapered round/octagonal steel
  pole ~8–9 m, cast "shoe" base with a handhole door, a davit / mast arm curving out 2.5–3 m over the
  roadway, an LED cobra-head (grey die-cast shell ~0.9 × 0.4 m, flat lens underneath). Seen in
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:400_First_Avenue.jpg and
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Broadway_nb_at_West_220th_Street,_Manhattan,_NY,_June_2026.jpg
- Spacing: roughly every 25–30 m along a kerb, staggered across the street.

## Traffic signals, pedestrian signals, street-name signs

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:400_First_Avenue.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Asia_Society_building,_Manhattan,_NY.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dyer_Avenue_sb_at_West_34th_Street,_Manhattan,_July_2025.jpg
  - Corner poles ~6–7 m, galvanised; signal heads have **federal-yellow housings**, three 12" (30 cm)
    lenses, tunnel visors; ~0.35 × 1.0 × 0.25 m. Side-mounted on the pole at ~3.5–4.5 m and/or hung
    from a mast arm reaching 4–6 m over the roadway.

  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pedestrian_signal,_Central_Park.jpg
  - Yellow box ~0.4 × 0.4 m, black face, LED orange hand / white walking person (countdown on newer),
    clamshell bracket; mounted ~2.5–3 m, one per crosswalk.
- Accessible push buttons: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pedestrian_signal_push_button_in_New_York.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pedestrian_signal_push_buttons_in_New_York.jpg
  - Yellow button housing at ~1.05 m with a white "PUSH BUTTON FOR AUDIBLE SIGNAL" plate above.
- Street-name signs: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bowling_Green_Plaza,_New_York_City_-_1783_Evacuation_Day_street_sign.jpg

## Fire hydrants

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Black_fire_hydrant_Manhattan_(w_56th_Street)_May_2023.JPG ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fire_Hydrant_on_East_101st_Street,_Lex_Avenue,_East_Harlem_Open_Streets,_June_15_2024,_East_Harlem,_Manhattan.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bollard,_bucket,_fire_hydrant,_trash_bin_at_the_northwest_end_of_Dyckman_Street_in_Inwood,_NYC.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fire_hydrant_New_York.jpg
  - ~0.75 m tall. Flanged base with bolts, plain barrel (r ≈ 9–10 cm), bolted bonnet flange at ~0.5 m,
    domed top, **pentagonal operating nut**, two 2½" side outlets with pentagon-nut caps and chains,
    often a 4½" front pumper outlet. Colours: black with silver top (very common now), red, yellow,
    red with white/silver top, occasional green.

## Street trees and pits

- London plane (Platanus × acerifolia), the most common NYC street tree:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bark_of_London_Plane_-_geograph.org.uk_-_7178146.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bark_of_the_London_Plane_-_geograph.org.uk_-_7373547.jpg ·
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Juharlevelu_platanus_(London_plane),_Bark,_Margaret_Island.jpg
  - Bark: exfoliating camouflage of olive, grey-brown and cream patches; trunk clear to ~3 m on
    streets, 3–5 ascending scaffold limbs, broad rounded crown 10–14 m tall on a typical block
    (much larger when old); palmate 3–5-lobed leaves 12–25 cm.
- Honey locust (thornless): open vase crown, fine pinnately compound leaves (small leaflets), dark
  furrowed bark; ~10–14 m. Callery pear / small ornamentals: short trunk, dense upright oval crown.
- Tree pits: rectangular, ~1.2 × 2.4 m (4 × 8 ft is typical; 5 × 10 ft on newer sidewalks) along
  the kerb, soil/mulch a little below the sidewalk; optional 18" (0.46 m) black steel guards with a
  top rail and hairpin loops (see the guard in
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fire_hydrant_New_York.jpg-style corner photos above).

## Openly licensed models considered

Poly Haven (CC0) has `fire_hydrant`, `street_lamp_01/02`, `metal_trash_can`, `trashbag`,
`water_manhole_cover`, `utility_box_01/02`, trees (`tree_small_02`, `island_tree_0x`,
`jacaranda_tree`). None matched the NYC references closely enough (South-African-style hydrant,
Victorian lanterns, galvanised dustbins) and the trees are 1–5 M triangles each, so the frequent
props are modelled procedurally to the dimensions above instead, and the trees use a small
procedural generator with painted leaf-card atlases (no external textures).
